Part I · The platform

What Display & Video 360 buys, and how

DV360 is a demand-side platform: it buys ad impressions from exchanges and publishers on the advertiser's behalf. It manages reservation, programmatic and programmatic guaranteed buys across display, video, TV, audio and other channels in one place, organized into five modules: Campaigns, Audiences, Creatives, Inventory and Insights.

How it's boughtPrice and volumeWhere it's set up in DV360
Open auctionReal-time bidding against other buyers. No guaranteed volume.The default: a line item with no inventory source targeting buys open auction on all enabled exchanges.
Private auction dealAn auction limited to invited buyers, with a floor price.Negotiated with the seller, then targeted explicitly in the line item. Deals are never bought unless targeted.
Preferred dealA fixed price, no guaranteed volume.Same: negotiated, then targeted. Automated bidding can be told to prioritize deals so it bids up to the floor or fixed price.
Programmatic guaranteedReserved volume at an agreed price.Bought through a default reservation line item. Budgets are fixed when the deal is created.
YouTube & Google inventoryAuction, or guaranteed through YouTube instant deals.YouTube & partners and Demand Gen line items, after accepting the partner-level terms.
Why one platform matters

Google says bringing reserved and open auction buying together lets an advertiser manage reach and frequency across all of it, and see exactly where the budget went and where ads ran. That's the core reason clients consolidate in DV360 rather than buying through several tools.

Partner, advertiser, campaign, insertion order, line item

Every DV360 question starts with "at which level?" Settings made higher up are inherited below, and some can't be changed lower down at all.

A Standard insertion order's objective (Brand awareness, Clicks, Conversions, Custom, or none) decides which KPIs, bid strategies and line item types it can hold. Part I · The platform

How the data works: bidding, AI, audiences, conversions and controls

Clients ask about these six topics more than any others. Open one to read it.

Automated bidding and learning time
What it does

Predicts how likely each impression is to lead to the chosen outcome (click, conversion or view) and whether it's available at a good price. Set at insertion order level (locks every line item) or line item level.

Two styles
  • Prioritize spending the full budget (Google's usual recommendation): raises bids until the budget spends, optionally capped by "Do not exceed average CPM".
  • Prioritize a target KPI: the goal comes first, so an aggressive target can leave budget unspent.
Timing

The algorithm can take up to 4 weeks to learn. Changes in the interface can take up to 24 hours to take effect, and big bid or targeting changes can cause spend spikes while it relearns.

Source: Automated bid strategies

Google AI: enhanced automation, optimized targeting, custom bidding
Enhanced automation

Bidding models trained on aggregated, anonymized click, conversion and impression data from participating advertisers, to fill signal gaps. A partner admin accepts the terms (all advertisers are then opted in by default); insertion orders can opt in for A/B tests. Allow 2 to 4 weeks. Not available for custom bidding or YouTube.

Optimized targeting

Starts from your chosen audiences and finds more people likely to convert. Geography, language, exclusions, brand safety and placements are always respected; audience inclusions are only signals, so ads can serve beyond them. Needs automated bidding on clicks, conversions or custom bidding. Not for CTV line items or viewability strategies.

Custom bidding

Bid toward impressions you value, using Floodlight, custom Floodlight variables, Google Analytics or impression signals. Rule-based is recommended over scripts. Not available on YouTube, programmatic guaranteed or Demand Gen. A model needs training data, and assigning an untrained model to a live line item stops it spending.

Source: Enhanced automation · Optimized targeting · Custom bidding overview

First-party audiences: Customer Match, Google-engaged audiences, PAIR
Customer Match
  • Upload hashed contact details or mobile device IDs (CSV, at least 100 members). Processing takes up to 3 hours.
  • Members expire after 540 days, so lists need regular refreshing.
  • Contact-info lists only serve on Google Ad Manager and YouTube inventory, and can't be used with HTML5 or rich media creatives or third-party trackers.
  • In the EEA, consent must be passed for ad personalization.
Google-engaged audiences

Automatic lists of people who clicked through to the site from Google properties. Only YouTube & partners and Demand Gen line items can target them.

PAIR

Publisher Advertiser Identity Reconciliation lets an advertiser and a publisher match their own first-party data through a clean room, without sharing user-level data with each other or with DV360. Generally available since 2025.

Source: Customer Match audience · Google-engaged audiences · Google blog: DV360 and PAIR (Jan 2025)

Floodlight conversions and which report to trust
Floodlight

The conversion tracking shared by Campaign Manager 360, DV360 and Search Ads 360. The Floodlight configuration lives in Campaign Manager 360. A conversion is counted when a tagged page fires after an ad view or click within the window; if there was a click, it counts as click-through.

Counting methods

Standard (every conversion), Unique (first per user per day), Per session, Transactions and Items sold.

YouTube and Demand Gen

With Floodlight optimization turned on at advertiser level, each line item picks its Floodlight activities and attribution model, and YouTube auto-tagging is forced on. Use the Standard report to compare with other line items, and the YouTube report to set target CPA. The two can differ: the YouTube report attributes to the ad interaction date and uses YouTube's engaged-view thresholds.

Source: About Floodlight · How Floodlight counts conversions · Floodlight optimization for YouTube and Demand Gen

Brand safety and suitability
Two different things

Brand safety: Google automatically filters content no advertiser wants. Brand suitability: each advertiser opts in or out of content some brands accept and others don't.

Settings
  • Set at advertiser level and applied to every line item: Maximum (default), Moderate or Limited inventory, plus content theme exclusions such as politics, religion or sensitive news.
  • Selected deals can be exempted from all advertiser suitability settings (not YouTube, Demand Gen or packages). Google's own safety filters still apply.
  • Third-party pre-bid verification: Integral Ad Science, DoubleVerify, Scope3. Not supported for audio.

Source: Brand suitability

Frequency caps across screens
Where

Campaign, insertion order or line item. A higher-level cap constrains everything below it, like a budget. The longest period is now 30 days: lifetime caps were removed in February 2025.

How

DV360 uses modeling and identifiers such as publisher-provided IDs, exchange-provided IDs and device advertising IDs. On connected TV, an impression with no supported ID is excluded from line items that use frequency caps.

Guaranteed buys

For programmatic guaranteed deals, campaign caps are best effort: DV360 prioritizes delivering the reservation. Demand Gen line items have no line item cap but respect campaign and insertion order caps.

Source: Manage frequency with frequency caps · Create a Demand Gen line item

Part II · Learn the language

Five things that will confuse you, and what to do

"My line item is active but isn't spending"
Why it happens

No approved creative yet; it's been less than 24 hours; a deal isn't actually targeted, or the bid is below its floor or fixed price; a target KPI is too aggressive; viewability targeting combined with Active View bidding; strict brand suitability or frequency caps.

What to do

Check creative approval, inventory source targeting and the bid strategy first, then the troubleshooter and Inventory Availability report. See Automated bid strategies: troubleshooting and Create a line item.

YouTube conversions differ between the Standard and YouTube reports
Why it happens

The Standard report attributes conversions to the conversion date and uses your Floodlight engaged-view settings. The YouTube report attributes to the ad interaction date and uses YouTube's minimum engaged-view time (10 seconds for skippable in-stream, 5 for Shorts and in-feed).

What to do

Use the Standard report for overall performance and comparisons; use the YouTube report to set target CPA. See Floodlight optimization for YouTube and Demand Gen.

"Ads are reaching people outside my audience list"
Why it happens

With optimized targeting on, audience inclusions are signals, not limits. It's turned on automatically for new automated-bidding line items that target affinity, in-market or custom audiences.

What to do

Report with the "Optimized targeting" dimension to split the impressions. If the client must stay inside a first-party list, turn optimized targeting off on that line item. See Optimized targeting.

A frequency-capped CTV line item barely delivers
Why it happens

On connected TV, impressions without a supported device or publisher ID are excluded from line items that use frequency caps. A low cap also cuts the impressions a line item can bid on.

What to do

Check the cap at every level (campaign and insertion order caps constrain the line item), and ask whether the publishers pass device IDs. See Frequency caps.

A Customer Match list shows a size but doesn't serve
Why it happens

Members older than 540 days no longer count, and the list needs at least 100 recent members. Contact-info lists don't serve on third-party exchanges, or with HTML5, rich media or third-party-tracked creatives.

What to do

Check when the list was last refreshed, the inventory sources and the creative types. See Customer Match audience.

Escalate nowA live line item uses a custom bidding model that isn't Ready
Why it happens

Google says assigning a model that's still Training to a live campaign makes the line item stop spending. A model with no spend for 21 days is Suspended and stops training.

What to do

Tell your DV360 lead right away, with the algorithm's status from Resources > Custom bidding. Don't swap bid strategies on a live client line item yourself. See Custom bidding overview.

Lab · Task 2 of 5 · Insertion orders and line items

Choose insertion orders, objectives and line item types.

Deliverable to record

Each insertion order: its type, objective, KPI and budget share, and the line item types inside it.

What you'll understand afterwards

How the insertion order objective limits what you can put inside it, and why channels often need separate insertion orders.

Hint
  • A Connected TV insertion order can only hold Connected TV and YouTube & partners on CTV line items.
  • YouTube & partners video line items fit a Brand awareness objective; Demand Gen needs Conversions, Clicks or no objective.
  • Automatic budget allocation works with Demand Gen only if the insertion order holds nothing but Demand Gen line items.
What a good answer looks like
  1. Connected TV IO · Brand awareness · KPI CPM or cost per unique co-view reach · Connected TV + YouTube & partners on CTV line items.
  2. Standard IO · Brand awareness · KPI such as viewability or completion rate · YouTube & partners video line items (for example efficient reach).
  3. Standard IO · Conversions · KPI CPA · Demand Gen line items only (so automatic budget allocation can be used), plus a separate conversions IO for display line items if you want both.

A strong answer splits the budget toward the goals (for example more early on awareness), explains why CTV sits in its own insertion order, and notes that YouTube line items can't go in a Conversions insertion order.

No single right answer Budget splits are judgment calls; discuss yours with your buddy.

See it in a real account

Open a live campaign's Insertion orders tab and look at the objective and optimization type of each one. Notice which line item types sit together.

Lab · Task 3 of 5 · Bidding and audiences

Pick bid strategies and AI settings.

Deliverable to record

For the conversions line items: the bid strategy, whether optimized targeting is on, how you use the 250,000 past buyers, and whether you'd use enhanced automation or custom bidding.

What you'll understand afterwards

Which AI features work together, which ones exclude each other, and how first-party data acts as a signal or a limit.

Hint
  • Northwind wants new boot buyers. Should past buyers be targeted, excluded, or used as a signal?
  • Custom bidding isn't available on Demand Gen; enhanced automation isn't available for custom bidding or YouTube.
  • Customer Match contact lists only serve on Google Ad Manager and YouTube inventory.
What a good answer looks like
  • Bid strategy: Maximize conversions while prioritizing spending the full budget, on the Northwind Floodlight purchase activity. Expect up to 4 weeks of learning, so don't judge in week 1.
  • Past buyers: upload them as a Customer Match list (with consent). For a prospecting line item, exclude them, which optimized targeting always respects; for a re-engagement line item, target them on Google and YouTube inventory.
  • Optimized targeting: on for prospecting display line items, with in-market or custom audiences as signals; Google reports average gains when it's used with enhanced automation.
  • Enhanced automation: a partner-level decision that pools anonymized data; ask your lead before suggesting it, and test it at insertion order level first.
  • Custom bidding: not needed here, and unavailable on Demand Gen. It would make sense later, for example to value high-margin products more using a custom Floodlight variable.
See it in a real account

In a live line item, look at the Bid strategy card and the Optimized targeting card. In Resources > Custom bidding, note each algorithm's status.

Lab · Task 4 of 5 · Controls

Set brand suitability and frequency caps.

Deliverable to record

Northwind's brand suitability settings and where they're set, plus a frequency cap plan: level, exposures and time period, and one CTV risk.

What you'll understand afterwards

Which controls apply to everything, which can be exempted, and how caps interact across levels and screens.

Hint
  • "No political or sensitive news" maps to content theme exclusions.
  • A campaign cap covers every insertion order below it; the longest period is 30 days.
  • What happens to CTV impressions with no device ID when a cap is set?
What a good answer looks like
  • Suitability: at advertiser level (Advertiser settings > Targeting rules), Moderate inventory as a balance of reach and suitability, with theme exclusions for Politics and News (sensitive). It applies to every line item and can't be changed lower down. If a negotiated CTV deal under-delivers because of it, the deal can be exempted, and Google's own safety filtering still applies.
  • Frequency: a campaign-level cap across all three insertion orders (for example a weekly cap), so one person isn't over-exposed across CTV, YouTube and display. Lower caps reduce what line items can bid on.
  • CTV risk: impressions without a supported ID are excluded from capped line items, so a tight cap can starve CTV delivery.

No single right answer The exact cap is a judgment call; Google doesn't recommend a number.

See it in a real account

Read an advertiser's Targeting rules > Brand suitability, then the frequency cap in a campaign's settings and an insertion order's Details tab.

Source: Brand suitability · Frequency caps

Lab · Task 5 of 5 · Testing discipline

Design an A/B experiment.

Deliverable to record

A one-page plan: the hypothesis, the arms, the single variable, the research goal, the confidence level, the split and budget, and the duration.

What you'll understand afterwards

How DV360 experiments split users, and the setup mistakes that make results meaningless.

Hint
  • Northwind's team asks: "Does optimized targeting find more boot buyers than our own audience choices?"
  • Experiments compare insertion orders or line items. Change one thing only.
  • Think about what happens to the frequency cap and budget when users are split.
What a good answer looks like
  • Hypothesis: "Display line items with optimized targeting on will deliver a lower CPA than the same line items with it off."
  • Arms: two line items made by duplicating, identical except optimized targeting; new line items, not ones with history; one is the baseline.
  • Goal and confidence: conversions, 95% confidence.
  • Split and budget: 50/50, with budgets proportional to the split and the same pacing. The insertion order cap must leave room for both arms. The split can't change once the experiment starts.
  • Duration: long enough for automated bidding to learn (up to 4 weeks), starting at least 24 hours after setup, ideally matching the line items' flight. To pause, pause the arms, never the experiment: an ended experiment can't restart.
See it in a real account

Open Experiments in an advertiser and look at an A/B test's arms, audience split and the Diff tab, which shows any differences between arms.

Source: Test ads with experiments · Optimized targeting · Automated bid strategies

Part III · Module 1 of 4

When does a client need Display & Video 360?

Your job is to recognize the needs DV360 is built for, ask the right questions, then bring in a consultant to confirm.

Signals that point to DV360
  • Buying across many exchanges and deals, including private auctions, preferred deals and programmatic guaranteed, in one place. Source
  • Negotiating directly with publishers through Marketplace and requests for proposal. Source
  • Connected TV, digital out-of-home or audio alongside display and YouTube. Source
  • Reach and frequency managed across reserved and auction buys. Source
  • Floodlight already in place through Campaign Manager 360, shared with Search Ads 360. Source
  • Brand controls and third-party verification (IAS, DoubleVerify, Scope3) applied to every buy. Source
When DV360 may not be the right fit
  • The client only buys YouTube and Google inventory and has no need for exchanges or deals.
  • Nobody on the client or agency side will manage it day to day.
  • Budgets are too small for the fee and the people time to pay off.
  • Unverified These are judgment calls, not Google statements. This site didn't check Google Ads' own Help Center; ask your DV360 lead before telling a client what Google Ads can or can't do.
Scenario

A streaming service is launching a new show. It wants connected TV ads with two broadcasters it already talks to, YouTube, and display retargeting of people who visited the show page. It uses Campaign Manager 360 and wants one frequency cap across everything.

What would you recommend?
Show the reasoning and the questions to ask

Why DV360 is likely: broadcaster deals can be negotiated or imported and bought alongside YouTube and display, and a campaign-level frequency cap applies across the insertion orders below it. Campaign Manager 360 use means Floodlight is likely in place for the retargeting and conversions.

What to find out before recommending:

  1. Do the broadcasters sell through an exchange DV360 supports, and as programmatic guaranteed or preferred deals?
  2. Do they pass device IDs? Without them, frequency-capped CTV impressions are excluded.
  3. Is the show page tagged with Floodlight, and who owns the configuration?
  4. Which countries? (For example, US YouTube audio is sold through SiriusXM.)
  5. Who will manage DV360 day to day: the client, an agency or a partner?

Bring in a technical consultant to confirm the setup before the recommendation goes to the client.

Sources: Negotiate a deal · Frequency caps · Connected TV insertion orders · About Floodlight

Part III · Module 2 of 4

Recognizing common client issues

You don't need to fix these. Ask good questions, check what you can, and know when to hand over. The causes listed are possibilities to investigate, not diagnoses.

1. "Our campaign is under-delivering"
What the client sees

Spend or impressions well behind plan.

Ask first
  • Which insertion orders and line items, since when?
  • Any recent changes to targeting, bids, creatives or brand suitability?
  • Are deals involved, and what are their floor or fixed prices?
Possible causes

Unapproved creatives; aggressive target KPIs; bids below deal prices; tight frequency caps or suitability settings; a new or changed bid strategy still learning.

You can check

Creative approval, bid strategy settings, inventory source targeting, frequency caps at every level.

Escalate when

Bids, budgets or targeting need changing, or a guaranteed deal is at risk.

Source: Automated bid strategies · Frequency caps

2. Conversions don't match Campaign Manager 360 or GA4
What the client sees

DV360 conversion totals differ from another tool.

Ask first
  • Same dates, filters and time zone?
  • Which Floodlight activities, attribution model and counting method?
  • For YouTube: Standard report or YouTube report?
Possible causes

Different attribution models or windows; view-through vs click-through counting; YouTube report timing and engaged-view thresholds; GA4 measuring site behavior, not ad-attributed Floodlight conversions.

You can check

Report settings, the line item's tracked Floodlight activities and attribution model.

Escalate when

A tag may not be firing, or the gap appeared suddenly.

Source: How Floodlight counts conversions · Floodlight optimization for YouTube

3. "Our ad appeared next to unsuitable content"
What the client sees

A screenshot of their ad next to content they consider off-brand.

Ask first
  • Where and when, and which ad? Open auction or a deal?
  • Which suitability level and theme exclusions are set?
  • Is that deal exempted from suitability settings?
Possible causes

Maximum inventory selected; a deal exemption; content outside the excluded themes; no third-party verification on that line item.

You can check

Advertiser Targeting rules, deal exemptions, the line item's verification settings.

Escalate when

Always: brand safety complaints go to your DV360 lead the same day.

Source: Brand suitability

4. "Why can't my team see costs?" or "Why can they see too much?"
What the client sees

Users see "No access" where cost should be, or see fees they shouldn't.

Ask first
  • Which users, which role, at partner or advertiser level?
  • What do they need to do?
Possible causes

Creative and Planner – limited roles hide cost data; the Partner client role hides fees and linked accounts; all of a user's roles must be in the same role family.

You can check

The user's roles under User management (if you're allowed).

Escalate when

Access needs changing. Only an Admin can change users.

Source: Manage users

5. "Our YouTube line items stopped" after a product change
What the client sees

Line items paused or archived, or new ones can't be saved.

Ask first
  • Which line item type and bid strategy?
  • When did it stop?
Possible causes

YouTube video action line items were archived on 28 February 2026 (replaced by Demand Gen); legacy maximum CPV and single-format efficient reach line items on 26 October 2026; new YouTube line items need a business name and logo from 12 October 2026.

You can check

The DV360 announcements page for the date.

Escalate when

Line items need rebuilding or migrating.

Part III · Module 3 of 4

Display & Video 360 enterprise architecture

Three building blocks come up in almost every enterprise conversation.

DV360

Partner and advertiser structure

A partner holds advertisers; each advertiser holds campaigns, insertion orders and line items.

Why organizations use it
  • Set currency, exchanges, fees and data sharing once at partner level.
  • Give each business its own advertiser, creatives, audiences and brand suitability.
Limitations
  • Admin is a partner-level role; only Admins manage users and billing.
  • Channels, first-party lists and deals belong to one advertiser and can't be moved with a Structured Data File.
  • Advertiser-level brand suitability can't be overridden lower down.
Scenario

An agency runs DV360 for several brands. It has one partner, one advertiser per brand, and gives each brand team roles on its own advertiser only.

Source: Partners · Manage users · Structured Data Files

DV360 + Campaign Manager 360

Floodlight and measurement

Floodlight tags record conversions for DV360, Campaign Manager 360 and Search Ads 360.

Why organizations use it
  • De-duplicate conversions across display and search.
  • Build audiences from site actions and feed automated and custom bidding.
  • Optimize YouTube and Demand Gen to the same Floodlight activities as other line items.
Limitations
  • The Floodlight configuration and lookback windows are managed in Campaign Manager 360.
  • Modeled conversions aren't in Floodlight reports.
Scenario

Automation and data sharing

Automated bidding, optimized targeting, enhanced automation and custom bidding work on top of the advertiser's data.

Why organizations use it
  • Fill signal gaps where third-party cookies aren't available.
  • Find new converters beyond existing lists.

  • Enhanced automation pools anonymized data; data shared before opting out stays in the pool.

Part III · Module 4 of 4

Understanding DV360 fees, revenue models and support

Clients often ask why their bill isn't the same as the media they bought. Three things explain most of it.

Platform

1. The DV360 fee

The rate a business pays to use DV360, a percentage of media spend, set by contract at partner level.

There's a higher rate for exchange inventory (open auction, private and preferred deals) and a lower rate for YouTube & partners, Demand Gen and programmatic guaranteed. Mobile app mediation partners add their own fee, which advertisers must accept.

Source: Partners: DV360 fee · App mediation partners

Costs

2. Partner and data costs

Total media cost = media cost + partner costs (such as the DV360 fee, third-party ad serving or verification) + data fees for third-party audiences.

Third-party verification prices are set by the vendors and can change without notice.

Source: Partner costs · Third-party verification

Agency

3. Revenue models

"Revenue" is what the agency charges the client. Total media cost markup adds a percentage to total media cost (the usual choice). CPM value charges a fixed CPM, which Google calls risky because it isn't tied to cost. Profit = revenue − total media cost.

Digital out-of-home deals can't use the CPM value model.

Source: Revenue models · Digital out-of-home

Scenario

A client asks: "Why is the Revenue column in our DV360 report higher than the media cost?"

Show a good answer

"Revenue in DV360 is what's charged for the line items, not just media. It's calculated from total media cost, which includes the platform fee, partner costs and any data fees, plus the agreed markup. For example, with a 20% markup on $20,000 of total media cost, revenue is $24,000."

Then offer to walk through the client's own revenue model and partner costs with your account team. Never quote fee rates or markups unless they're written in that client's agreement.
